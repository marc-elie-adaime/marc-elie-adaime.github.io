Decoding evolutionarily and ecologically signal from plant morphology and molecular data.

About

A path from the Arctic to plant evolution — held together by computation.

My work sits at the interface of evolutionary biology, ecology, and computational biology. I ask how biodiversity and traits change across environmental gradients and over evolutionary time, and I build the quantitative tools needed to read that history from incomplete and complex biological records.

I trained first as a Quaternary paleoecologist in Québec, reconstructing postglacial environments from Arctic lake sediments. My doctoral work turned to deep learning, training neural networks to extract phylogenetic and ecological signal from pollen morphology. At the Smithsonian, I now integrate the machine-learning methods we develop with forest-plot occurrence data and molecular and anatomical evidence to ask how plant lineages diversify, how they adapt to particular climatic zones, and why certain morphological traits arise — with much of this work focused on conifers.

Current directions

Three active threads

How do organisms and ecosystems record their own history, and how do we recover it accurately?

01 / ARCTIC

Paleoecology of northern lakes

Reconstruction of postglacial environmental change from High Arctic lake sediments, through microfossils, geochemistry, and eDNA, as proxies for millennia of ecological history and climatic variability.

02 / EVOLUTION

How and why plant clades diversify

Phylogenetics, evolutionary simulation, and trait analysis in plant groups like conifers, to understand why lineages evolve particular traits and what drives that change, from environmental pressures to selection to changes in the genome.

03 / METHODS

Morphology as overlooked evidence of evolution

New statistical and computational methods, including advanced signal processing and deep learning, for quantifying complex morphological traits in structures such as pollen, leaves, and cones, making such traits a rigorous source of phylogenetic inference.
