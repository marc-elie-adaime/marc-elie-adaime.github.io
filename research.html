Research

Recovering ecological and evolutionary history from imperfect records.

My research combines paleoecology, phylogenetics, and machine learning. The unifying goal is to read biodiversity dynamics, trait evolution, and environmental change out of the fragmentary evidence organisms leave behind — sediments, sequences, and the shapes of microscopic structures.

Training & trajectory

Where the questions came from

  • 2014 – 2019 · Université Laval, Québec

    Quaternary paleoecology in the Arctic

    Aquatic Paleoecology Laboratory · Centre d'études nordiques

    I began in physical geography and Quaternary science, reconstructing postglacial climate and landscape change from the sediments of Canada's northernmost lakes. My M.Sc. provided the first deglaciation chronology for the Fury and Hecla Strait region of Nunavut and evidence for a postglacial marine reconnection — work built on microfossils, geochemical proxies, and 3D bathymetric mapping.

  • 2019 – 2024 · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

    Ph.D. — deep learning for pollen morphology

    Punyasena Lab · National Center for Supercomputing Applications

    My doctoral research taught neural networks to extract phylogenetic and environmental information from the morphology of pollen. Using superresolution imaging and deep learning, I developed approaches to place fossil morphotypes phylogenetically and to reconstruct grassland diversity and photosynthetic composition across tens of thousands of years.

  • 2025 – present · Smithsonian Institution

    Postdoctoral Research Fellow, Data Science Lab

    Office of Digital & Innovation · Washington, D.C.

    I now work at the interface of ecology, evolution, and data science — integrating phenotypic, molecular, and environmental data to study adaptation, trait evolution, and biodiversity across gradients, and implementing large-scale models on high-performance computing infrastructure.

  • From January 2027 · American University of Beirut

    Assistant Professor, Bioinformatics & Computational Biology

    Department of Biology · Faculty of Arts and Sciences

    An incoming faculty position where I will build a program extending these methods to the biodiversity of Lebanon and the eastern Mediterranean.

Current research

Three active threads

01 / ARCTIC PALEOECOLOGY

Northern lakes as archives

I remain active in Arctic research, reconstructing millennia of environmental change from lake-sediment records. This work draws on microfossil and geochemical proxies, age–depth modeling, and increasingly on environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding to track how far-northern ecosystems responded to past climate — and how resilient they proved to be.

02 / EVOLUTION & PHYLOGENETICS

How plant lineages diversify

In parallel, I use molecular sequence data and phylogenetic reconstruction to study the diversification of important plant clades — with particular attention to conifers, including the evolution of fleshy cones in Podocarpaceae. Combining phylogenetic comparative methods with trait and genomic data, I ask how innovations and environments drive the buildup of diversity over evolutionary time.

03 / COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

Making morphology quantitative

Across both threads, I develop new statistical and computational methods for representing complex morphological data — with a special focus on plants, from pollen grains to cones. Deep learning on superresolution imagery lets shape itself become a rigorous variable, enabling phylogenetic placement, diversity estimation, and inference of ecological attributes such as C3/C4 photosynthetic pathway. These tools are released openly for the community.
